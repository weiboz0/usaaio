# Problem 3.

**Total: 15 points.**

## Part 3.1 (5 points)

Reasoning is not required.
Coding is not allowed.

Let

\[
S=\begin{pmatrix}7&-4\\3&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Exactly one of the following numbered pairs \((\lambda,q)\) is an eigenpair of \(S\):

1. \(\lambda=3,\quad q=\begin{pmatrix}4\\3\end{pmatrix}\)
2. \(\lambda=0,\quad q=\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\end{pmatrix}\)
3. \(\lambda=-4,\quad q=\begin{pmatrix}-4\\-3\end{pmatrix}\)
4. \(\lambda=4,\quad q=\begin{pmatrix}4\\3\end{pmatrix}\)
5. \(\lambda=7,\quad q=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\end{pmatrix}\)

Identify the eigenpair by checking \(Sq=\lambda q\), and use its eigenvalue \(\lambda\).
Write \(\lambda\) in the unique form \(p/q_0\), where \(p\) and \(q_0\) are integers, \(q_0>0\), and \(\gcd(|p|,q_0)=1\).
What is \(p+q_0\)?

- **A.** \(-3\)
- **B.** \(1\)
- **C.** \(4\)
- **D.** \(5\)
- **E.** \(8\)

## Part 3.2 (10 points)

Reasoning is required.
Coding is not allowed.

Using the dependent-rows route, find both eigenvalues of

\[
S=\begin{pmatrix}11&13\\-2&-4\end{pmatrix}.
\]

That is, determine the values of \(\lambda\) for which the two rows of \(S-\lambda I\) are linearly dependent.
Show the resulting quadratic equation and solve it.
List the eigenvalues in descending order.
Do not use determinant vocabulary.
