## Shared setup (used by every part of Problem 3)

**Encoder posterior and prior.** For an input row $x\in\mathbb R^{D}$ the encoder outputs a mean $\mu\in\mathbb R^{k}$ and a log-variance vector $\ell=\texttt{logvar}\in\mathbb R^{k}$, defining
$$q(z\mid x)=N\big(\mu,\operatorname{diag}(e^{\ell_1},\dots,e^{\ell_k})\big),\qquad p(z)=N(0,I_k).$$
All logarithms are natural.

**KL to the prior.** For one example,
$$\mathrm{KL}\big(q(z\mid x)\,\|\,p(z)\big)=\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{k}\big(\mu_j^2+e^{\ell_j}-\ell_j-1\big).$$

**Reparameterization.** Given noise $\varepsilon\in\mathbb R^k$, the latent is $z=\mu+\exp(\tfrac12\ell)\odot\varepsilon$.

**Decoder and reconstruction term.** The decoder maps $z$ to $\hat x\in\mathbb R^D$, read as the mean of $N(\hat x, I_D)$.
With the constant $\frac D2\ln 2\pi$ dropped, the per-example reconstruction term is
$$\mathrm{recon}=\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{D}(x_j-\hat x_j)^2\qquad(\text{a sum over features}).$$

**The β-weighted objective.** For a KL weight $\beta>0$, the per-example objective to **minimize** is
$$\mathcal L_\beta=\mathrm{recon}+\beta\,\mathrm{KL},$$
and the batch objective is the **mean over examples** of $\mathcal L_\beta$.
$\beta$ multiplies the KL term only.
For $\beta=1$ this is the negative ELBO; for $\beta\neq1$ it is a different objective and is reported as a β-weighted objective, not as an ELBO.
**Throughout Problem 3, $\beta=2$** unless a part states otherwise.
