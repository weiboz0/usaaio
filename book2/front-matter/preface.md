# About This Book

This book prepares you for Round 2 of the USA AI Olympiad.
It continues where *Machine Learning for the AI Olympiad* ends.
That book took you from Calculus AB and basic Python to neural networks, PyTorch, and the classical models of Round 1.
This one assumes all of it and goes further.

The book has six units.
The first three are about Transformers: how attention works, how language models are built and trained, and how the same ideas carry over to images, detection, and graphs.
The next two are about generative models: latent-variable models such as the variational autoencoder, then adversarial networks and diffusion models.
The last unit is a capstone in open-ended modeling.
It teaches you to plan an experiment under a budget, evaluate a model honestly, learn from mostly unlabeled data, solve a scientific inverse problem, and move a training run onto a GPU.

Round 2 rewards a different kind of work from Round 1.
Many questions are open-ended: you get data and a goal, build the best model you can within the rules, and explain your choices in a short writeup.
You will practice that skill throughout, not only at the end.
Every model in this book is small enough to train on a laptop CPU in seconds or minutes, so you can run every experiment yourself and see what each idea does.

Each unit begins with a short diagnostic of the Book 1 ideas it relies on.
If a diagnostic question is hard for you, follow its link back to the Book 1 unit and review that unit before you go on.
The diagnostic is not a test; it tells you where to start.

As in Book 1, every problem is original.
The problems follow the style and difficulty of the real competition, but none is copied from a past test.
Every dataset is either written out in full in a notebook or made by a short script with a fixed seed, so nothing in the course depends on downloads or hidden files.

At the end of the course, a two-day mock test lets you put everything together under exam conditions.
