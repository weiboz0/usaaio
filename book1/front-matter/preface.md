# About This Book

This book prepares you for Round 1 of the USA AI Olympiad.
It starts from two things you probably already have: Calculus AB and some basic Python.
Everything else is taught here, one idea at a time, before any problem asks you to use it.

The first part of the book builds the mathematical and programming foundations.
You will work with NumPy arrays, vectors and matrices, gradients of functions of several variables, probability, the singular value decomposition, and convex optimization.
The second part turns those tools into machine learning.
You will fit linear models, train them by gradient descent, build neural networks by hand and then in PyTorch, and study convolutional networks, word embeddings, dimensionality reduction, and the classical models such as support vector machines, decision trees, and k-means.
One unit is about competition craft itself: how to write a notebook that runs cleanly, respect an exact function contract, give answers in the requested form, and explain your approach in a short writeup.

Each unit is written as a short course of its own.
It opens with an overview, teaches its ideas over several class sessions, and closes with a review.
Inside every session you meet a motivating problem, a definition, a worked example, and checkpoints that ask you to try the idea right away.
Each unit then gives you between eighteen and thirty practice problems, grouped into three sets that grow from drills to exam-style questions to longer challenges.

The problems are original.
They are written to match the style and difficulty of the real competition, but none of them is copied from a past test.
Many of them are graded the way the competition grades work: an exact answer form, an exact function name, and sometimes a list of tools you may not use.
Learning to meet those requirements is part of the training.

You do not need to finish this book in a hurry.
The course schedule spreads it over two semesters, about forty weeks.
Work steadily, redo the problems that went wrong, and use the review at the end of each unit to find your weak spots.
At the end of the course, a full-length mock test lets you try everything under exam conditions.

When you are ready to go beyond Round 1, the companion book, *Deep Learning for the AI Olympiad*, continues with Transformers, generative models, and open-ended modeling for Round 2.
